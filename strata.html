Staying compliant

The pool rules, in plain English

Here's what NSW expects of an owners corporation with a pool, and where we help.

  • The owners corporation is responsible

    A pool on common property is jointly owned by all the lot owners, and the owners corporation is responsible for keeping it compliant with the Swimming Pools Act 1992.

  • Council inspections every three years

    Councils must inspect pools at properties with more than two dwellings at least once every three years, unless there's a current certificate of compliance. We flag gate, latch and fence issues as we see them, so there are no surprises.

  • Keep the CPR sign current

    A resuscitation sign must be displayed near the pool and readable from 3 metres away. If yours is faded or out of date, we'll let you know.

  • Holiday-let buildings

    If units are let to holiday guests, the pool may count as a public pool under NSW health rules, with stricter testing and record keeping. Your council can confirm which rules apply.

General information, not legal advice. Sources: NSW Government, Central Coast Council, NSW Health.

Are we locked into a contract?

No. Strata pool care is month to month, so you can stop any time. We keep the work by keeping the pool right.

How often should a strata pool be serviced?

It depends on how busy the pool is. Busy communal pools usually need a weekly visit through summer, and many can drop to fortnightly in the cooler months. We'll recommend a schedule after the site inspection.

Do you report to the strata manager?

Yes. After every visit you get a short report with the water readings, what we did and anything that needs attention, ready to pass on to the committee.

Can you quote for the committee?

Yes. After a free site inspection we'll put a fixed monthly price in writing that you can table at the next meeting.

Which areas do you cover?
